**Labels.**
- **Type**: C = Culture (memes, press explainers, Google Trends, TikTok and YouTube). E = Offline kid culture (toy lists, costumes, back-to-school).
- **Confirmed**: the fantasy shows up in 3 or more independent source *types* within the last 30 days (since 2026-10-01).
- **STALE**: the source is more than 60 days old (before 2026-09-01).
- I count the Google Frightgeist list as **C** (it is Google search-trend data). I count retailer, NRF and Spirit costume lists as **E**.

## Timing and runway per fantasy

Assumed ship date: **about Dec 1 to 12, 2026** (4 to 6 weeks from today), so the game lands right before winter break.

1. **Demon-hunting pop idol.** Late stage: the film came out June 2025 and "what is KPDH" explainers are everywhere. Even so, toys are only now reaching shelves for the 2026 holidays, so the gift wave (Dec 25) and post-Christmas play keep it alive. **Runway: about 3 to 6 months** (strong through Jan to Feb 2027, then a slow fade; the sequel is not until 2029). **IP:** Netflix/Sony licence. Do not use names, characters or the song "Golden". A generic "idol demon hunter" with a mic-sword and K-pop stage visuals is the only safe version.
2. **Squishy / sensory.** The August press explainers are a late signal, and so is Frightgeist No. 2. The Labubu comparison (it peaked summer 2025 and was declared "over" by Feb–Mar 2026, about 7 months later) suggests **runway of about 2 to 5 months**, with a peak at Christmas gifting and a fade in Q1 2027. **IP:** "NeeDoh" is Schylling's trademark, so call them generic "squishies". There is also a microwave/burn-lawsuit headline; avoid any heating mechanic.
3. **Mascot horror.** This is a multi-year genre (FNAF, Poppy Playtime, Digital Circus, now *Buddy*), so it is less a spike than a stable base. Halloween demand drops after today, but the genre stays. **Runway: 6+ months (evergreen-ish).** **Risk:** *Buddy* is R-rated with violence involving children, so do not reference it. Use original, cute-creepy mascots and keep the scares mild for ages 8 to 14.
4. **Superhero.** Evergreen. *Avengers: Doomsday* on Dec 18 is a winter-break spike. **Runway: evergreen; extra boost Dec to Jan.** Licensed IP, so stay generic.
5. **Creature collecting.** Evergreen, and the 30th anniversary gives a holiday boost. Gen 10 is not until 2027. **Runway: evergreen.** Use original creatures only.
6. **Hatch a sea monster or kaiju.** Pre-peak: the toy launched Aug 18 and is being pitched as the "holiday it-toy", so kids unbox it Dec 25. **Runway: about 3 to 6 months from ship** (rising into Christmas). It suits a "do one action" loop well (hatch an egg, reveal, collect rarer predators). Generic sharks and dinosaurs carry no IP risk.
7. **Witch / spellcaster.** *Hexed* opens Nov 25. Disney's November originals usually hold through winter break. **Runway: about 2 to 3 months**, depending on reviews.
8. **Pop star.** The Google kids' list is mostly adult-artist nostalgia, so it is a weak signal for game theming. Fold it into No. 1.
9. **Toys come alive.** Peaked June to July. It reaches Disney+ around the holidays, which may bring a small revival. **Runway: short.**
10. **Brainrot.** Saturated. There are many "Steal a ___ Brainrot" clones, press explainers date back to 2025, and nothing fresh appeared in October. **Runway: near zero for a new entrant.** Risks: an active trademark lawsuit (Mementum Lab holds an EU mark on Tung Tung Tung Sahur), and the characters are AI-made, which collides with the "that's so AI" insult.

## Anime-specific section

**Verdict: FLAT among kids aged 8 to 14, rising among teens 13 and up. It is not a rising *theme* in 8 to 14 offline culture in fall 2026.**

- **Offline (E):** No 2026 kids' costume list I found names a Japanese anime character except **Ash Ketchum (Pokémon), No. 5 on Google's kids' list**. Demon Slayer's kids' costume peak was Halloween 2025 (*Infinity Castle* Part 1). In 2026, the anime-*styled* phenomenon kids actually wear is **KPop Demon Hunters**, which is American/Korean animation with anime-like action. One costume retailer says anime picks (Attack on Titan, Demon Slayer) are mainly *teen* picks.
- **Roblox (C):** Anime combat is still a pillar on Roblox: Blox Fruits is No. 2 all-time, Jujutsu Shenanigans is a leading battlegrounds game, and analysts say "roleplay and anime combat dominate". So kids *play* anime-flavoured Roblox games even when they are not *watching* the source anime. That is a platform-native taste, not a fresh cultural spike.
- **Survey data:** There is no under-13 survey. About 60% of 13–17-year-olds call themselves anime fans (Crunchyroll/NRG, **May 2025, STALE**). Crunchyroll's COO says Gen Alpha "over-indexes". The US anime audience has doubled since 2020 (GEM 2026 White Paper, ages 13+).
- **Hot titles with this age group now, ranked by kid relevance:**
  1. **Pokémon**: 30th anniversary, TCG sell-outs in Sept 2026, Ash in the kids' top 5.
  2. **Demon Slayer**: residual; Part 1 on Crunchyroll since 2026-07-28; Part 2 not before 2027; possible Jump Festa news in Dec.
  3. **Jujutsu Kaisen**: mostly via Roblox battlegrounds; Common Sense rates it 12+; official Culling Game title due by end of 2026.
  4. **Dragon Ball**: the *DBS: Beerus* remake airs this Fall 2026 season, the most kid-safe current anime.
  5. **One Piece**: live-action Season 2 down about 30% and skews 18–34, so it is weak for ages 8–14.
- **Implication for an R6 game:** "Anime" works as a *style layer* (aura, power-up bursts, speed lines, sword slashes) on a rising non-anime fantasy, such as "demon hunter idol" or "hatch a monster". It is weak as the headline theme.

## Audience-sentiment and IP caveats

- **"That's so AI"** is Gen Alpha's "biggest insult" (Guardian, 2026-09-24, via Language Log), alongside "clanker". AI-looking art, AI voices or brainrot-style AI characters risk being dismissed as cheap or fake. Hand-made, blocky R6 art is an advantage here; say so in thumbnails and icons.
- **Meme-creator IP:** In the Italian brainrot fight, Mementum Lab is suing over Tung Tung Tung Sahur (counter-suit against Spyder/Steal a Brainrot; EU trademark granted; about 20 applications). Tung Tung was removed and later reinstated in Steal a Brainrot. Epic licensed it from Brainrot Factory. **Do not use named brainrot characters.**
- **Licensed IP:** KPop Demon Hunters (Netflix/Sony), NeeDoh (Schylling), Pokémon, Marvel, Toy Story, Primal Hatch (Spin Master / Jurassic World), Godzilla (Toho/Bandai) and *Buddy* are all protected. Use only generic versions: idol demon hunter, squishies, creature trainer, hero, sea monster egg.
- **Parent and safety risk:** *Buddy* is R-rated; mascot horror draws parental concern; the NeeDoh heating trend led to a lawsuit. Keep horror mild and avoid any "heat or microwave it" mechanic.
- **Evidence quality:** All rows come from search snippets because WebFetch was blocked. Several results came from SEO or spam domains and were excluded (geosirius.ifz.ru, presentationbuilder.*, featurespace.com, media-azi.md). The NRF 2026 counts come from a prediction-market comment and are unverified. The Angry Birds 3 date conflicts between sources. The Google Frightgeist list measures year-on-year *growth*, not volume, and it includes adults.
- **Missing data:** I could not see the Know Your Meme October 2026 nominees or the "Great Meme Reset of 2026" details, and I found no October Roblox concurrent-player figures for brainrot games.
